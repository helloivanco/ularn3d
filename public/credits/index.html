Credits & history

Ularn 3D stands on classic shoulders.

This page honors the creators and maintainers of Larn and Ultra-Larn. Dates and roles follow public histories such as larn.org’s History of Larn and Ularn by Derek Newhall, RogueBasin, and the Ularn SourceForge project notes.

Larn

Noah Morgan’s caves, 1986.

Noah Morgan created Larn for Unix and shared it for others to enjoy. The adventure asks you to find a cure for your ailing daughter before time runs out — exploring a persistent town, a multi-level dungeon, and volcanic depths beneath.

Morgan’s last major authored line is commonly cited as Larn 11.0; Larn 12.0 (August 1986) is associated with Morgan and James D. McNamara, who handled distribution and patching. Morgan’s terms allowed free use and modification so long as the work was not sold for profit.

Ports and follow-ons kept Larn alive on other systems, including Don Kneller’s MS-DOS port and later DOS work by Kevin Routley. Variants and rewrites continue; Morgan’s original also remains part of the NetBSD games collection.

Ultra-Larn

Phil Cordier’s Ularn.

Phil Cordier created Ultra-Larn — usually shortened to Ularn — while at UC Santa Cruz. The copyright date is 1987; the first wide Usenet posting is often dated 1989. Ularn grew from Larn 12.0 and introduced true character classes, additional dungeon and volcano levels, more demon lords, new artifacts, and other expansions that define the Ularn flavor many players know.

Cordier continued the line through Ularn 2.0 (“The Addiction of Ularn”) into the 1990s. Later packaging and maintenance included David Richerby (Ularn 1.5.1), Josh Brandt (1.5.2–1.5.4), and Josh Bressers (maintainer from 2005). Julian Olds produced a Windows-oriented rewrite often called Ultra Larn 1.6. Scott S. Snyder’s MS-DOS patch is among the early Ularn ports.

We thank Cordier and everyone who shepherded Ultra-Larn. This 3D project is a respectful presentation of that tradition, not a claim of authorship over their work.

Timeline

A short path to this edition.

  1. 1986 Larn

    Noah Morgan releases Larn on Unix; Larn 12.0 becomes the root of later branches.

  2. 1986–1991 Ports and PC Larn

    Don Kneller, Kevin Routley, and others bring Larn to DOS and other platforms.

  3. 1987–1990s Ultra-Larn / Ularn

    Phil Cordier expands Larn into Ularn with classes, deeper levels, and new content.

  4. 2000s Continued Ularn maintenance

    David Richerby, Josh Brandt, Josh Bressers, and others keep Ularn building and playable.

  5. Today This Ularn 3D edition

    Rules and adventure powered by Jason Primeau’s JavaScript Larn / Ularn engine, presented in an overhead Three.js world with browser play and an optional Windows build.
