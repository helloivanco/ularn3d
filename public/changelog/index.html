Recent versions

From the current line.

Maintained alongside CHANGELOG.md in the repository. Older tags remain on GitHub Releases.

  1. v1.8.1

    Clear sight in the caves

    • On Windows, heroes, skeletons and every chest, barrel, crate, candle and torch show their colors again. Since 1.6.0 the desktop app had drawn them plain white.
    • In Cinematic, the caves are as easy to read as Auto: a soft light fills every corner you have explored, and your lantern, the torches and the candles add warm pools on top. The dark edge around the screen is lighter.
    • In the Cinematic town, the knight’s silver no longer burns white in the sun.
    • Walls lower in front of any creature, item, chest, staircase or fountain you know about, not only in front of your hero, so nothing hides behind them.
    • The caves open on the wider view again, so you see more around you. Your cave zoom is reset once.
    • The first time you find a way up or down on a floor, a notice tells you, and your map glows in the color of the arrow that now points to it.
    • Wall bricks run in level courses from block to block, and wall tops are paved in one direction instead of a patchwork.
    • Stairs up are marked green and stairs down red, matching their map symbols and compass arrows.
    • The classic rules are unchanged.
  2. v1.8.0

    The whole floor at a glance

    • The map in the corner now shows the whole floor at once. It no longer scrolls with you: your gold marker moves across it, and ground you have not explored stays dark.
    • Once you have seen a way up or down, a green arrow (up) or red arrow (down) points from you toward the nearest one, like a compass. It turns as you walk. Dead ends get no arrow, and the Expand map shows the same arrows.
    • In Cinematic, creatures, your hero and the things you find no longer burn white beside a wall torch or under your lantern. They keep their colors, faces and shading.
    • In Cinematic, a creature standing between you and the camera no longer shows a black front, while the caves keep their darkness away from the light.
    • On short phone screens the map stays a slim strip, so the destination menu is never covered.
    • The classic rules are unchanged.
  3. v1.7.2

    Candlelight and a breeze

    • Candles in the caves burn: every wick has a small flame that sways, stretches and brightens on its own beat, with a soft glow around it.
    • In Cinematic, the candles nearest you light their corner with a warm, wavering glow, alongside your lantern and the wall torches.
    • In Cinematic, the town’s lawns grow a thicker meadow, and the grass, wildflowers and bushes sway in a breeze that rolls across the town.
    • Cinematic keeps flames and grass moving smoothly while you stand still. Reduced motion holds them still.
    • The caves open closer on your hero, so they no longer look small on entering. Your earlier zoom in the caves is reset once to the new view.
    • Your hero no longer flashes in as the old character before the new one loads. The title starts loading your hero while you choose, so play opens with them ready.
    • The calling portraits on the title screen and the end-of-run recap show the new heroes.
    • Re-centering the camera in the caves no longer changes your zoom in town.
    • Entering a cave in Cinematic no longer logs graphics errors. The classic rules are unchanged.
  4. v1.7.1

    Fewer dead ends

    • When the only way on is a closed door, the next step walks you to it instead of telling you to explore. Exploring still never opens doors for you, since a door can shock or weaken whoever opens it.
    • Beside a single closed door, the next step reads “Open the door” with its direction, and one click opens it.
    • Opening a door or aiming a spell offers eight direction buttons instead of unrelated pack commands. The spell list is titled Cast a spell.
    • Badly hurt with nothing hostile in sight, the next step offers Rest, which waits until you recover and wakes you if a creature appears. It is the classic M command, now in the Field Guide too.
    • In stores, the college and the trading post, point at a listed choice to light it and click to choose it.
    • Every item in your pack shows its picture, including your starting gear, a continued expedition and anything you buy.
    • Continuing an expedition opens the journal at the latest entry instead of the oldest.
    • Town destinations use the names on the building signs, and arrow keys move your hero again right after you pick one.
    • Settings checkboxes sit beside their labels.
    • Co-op scores from runs that use the pack menu verify again once the score service is updated. The classic rules are unchanged.
  5. v1.7.0

    Lantern-lit caves and a pack at hand

    • Click any item in your pack to use it. A small menu offers what it can do: drink, read, eat, wear, wield or drop. Arrow keys move through the menu and each choice’s own key picks it.
    • If an item of the same kind lies at your feet, you are asked to step off it first, so the one used is the one from your pack. It works the same in multiplayer rooms.
    • The rats along the cave walls are animated 3D rats that sniff, groom, rear up and scurry from tile to tile.
    • Bestiary cards no longer squash together or overlap, and creatures you haven’t met yet show as clean silhouettes.
    • In Cinematic, the caves are lit for real. Your lantern lights the stone around you, and every creature, chest and pillar casts a shadow.
    • The nearest wall torches flicker, and their light stops at the walls instead of reaching into the next corridor. Away from any light, rooms fade into darkness.
    • Underground, armor and blades catch a faint glint of distant torchlight instead of the town’s sky. In the volcano, the cracks in the basalt glow on their own, beyond your lantern.
    • In Cinematic, the town is set in late afternoon. A low golden sun throws long shadows from the buildings, lamp posts and trees, and the shade takes on the sky’s blue.
    • Balanced and Auto look as before. The classic rules are unchanged.
    • The website has new screenshots from the lit caves and the town, a portrait of every calling, and a new social card.
  6. v1.6.0

    Living heroes and a weekly challenge

    • The title screen has a Weekly challenge: everyone who plays it this week explores the same town, store and dungeon. A new one starts every Monday at 00:00 UTC.
    • Each week sets the calling and difficulty for everyone, whatever you picked for your own expeditions.
    • Your first weekly run on this browser is the one that counts. Continue picks it up like any expedition, and later runs that week are practice that is never recorded.
    • The weekly board ranks this week’s runs and marks yours. During a weekly run, the in-game scoreboard shows it too.
    • Every weekly floor is ready before you set out, so nothing you do on the way down changes what waits there.
    • Your hero is now a fully animated character for every calling: idle, walking and running, sword swings, spellcasting, flinching and falling.
    • Zombies, wraiths and vampires rise as animated skeletons.
    • On wide windows the camera keeps your hero at the center of the screen instead of off to the right of the map and inventory panels.
    • Stairs going down are a stairwell sunk into the floor, and stairs going up are solid steps with railings. Nothing floats above them any more, and dead-end rubble no longer looks like spikes.
    • Cave corners and dead ends gather barrels, crates, kegs, candles and rubble, and torches burn on the walls. None of it blocks your way or hides loot.
    • Chests are banded wooden chests.
    • The town has lamp posts along its paths, barrels and crates by the shops, and wildflowers, bushes and stones across the lawns.
    • In Cinematic, wall torches throw warm pools of light across the stone and onto you, burning redder in the volcano. Walls cast a soft shade onto the floor and torch flames flicker.
    • The first swing on a floor no longer hitches while effects warm up. The classic rules are unchanged.
  7. v1.5.0

    Sights, sound and a next step

    • Every surface is repainted in far finer detail: hand-cut flagstones that never repeat across a room, laid wall courses, a meadow of grass, overlapping slate roofs and weathered planks. The volcano floor is now cooled basalt with glowing cracks.
    • In Cinematic, the stones near you catch your lantern light on their edges.
    • Every trap looks like what it is: arrow plates, trapdoors, teleport rune circles, dart traps and pits. Thrones show their jewels until you pry them off, mirrors reflect, and the sphere of annihilation is a black void.
    • Altars, statues, chests and the gelatinous cube are remodelled. McDope’s wares and the amulet of life preservation have their own models, and the cure potion glows once you know it.
    • Loot rests on the floor with a soft shadow. Diamonds, blades and pale potions are no longer missing pieces, and rings lose the white patch in their middle.
    • Ularn has a new original orchestral score: strings, horns, woodwinds, harp, celesta and choir, built around one theme you will start to recognise.
    • The town plays a pastoral tune with something uneasy underneath. The caves are a slow hymn of stone and shadow, and from floor 8 the deep halls grow darker still. The volcano churns with an ominous riff and low brass.
    • The music rises as danger nears: a pulse when a creature is close, then brass and timpani when you are surrounded or trading blows.
    • Short orchestral cues mark levelling up, each new depth you reach, finding the cure, death and victory.
    • Fountains, altars and thrones have their own sounds, and spells and stairs vary a little each time.
    • Music still loads one region at a time and plays offline in the desktop app.
    • A prompt under your hero shows the next step: what the tile underfoot allows and its key, a creature in reach to attack, or where to head next, such as the stairs you have found, the caves, or home with the cure. Click or tap it to do it.
    • The title answers Enter at once. Continue names your saved hero, their depth and level, and the time they have left.
    • Going deeper than any earlier expedition is marked on the floor card and with a notice.
    • Controllers work on every screen. The stick or d-pad moves and attacks, A takes the next step, X explores, Y opens your pack and Menu pauses. In menus the d-pad moves between buttons and A chooses. The Field Guide lists every button.
    • Keyboard focus shows a gold ring, and the run recap takes focus so Tab reaches Try again first.
    • On phones, notices and messages sit above the new prompt instead of over it.
    • The recap no longer names a hero’s calling twice when the hero is named after it, and says “1 mobul”. The classic rules are unchanged.
  8. v1.4.0

    Every blow lands

    • Hits flash the creature and knock it back, damage numbers rise from the blow, and a nameplate shows how wounded your foe is, never its exact health.
    • Heavy blows shake the view and pause for a heartbeat. Slain creatures topple and sink, and the loot they drop springs out of the floor.
    • Spells burst where they land. Levelling up raises a column of light and a banner listing what you gained.
    • When you are hurt, the edges of the screen redden and your health bar shows what you lost. At low health a heartbeat sounds and the music dulls.
    • Each new floor fades in with its depth, and tells you if you fell through a trap door.
    • Death plays out before a new recap: who killed you, how deep you reached, your kills and turns, and how the run compares with your best. Press R to try again with the same hero at once.
    • Your records, 23 feats and a bestiary of all 66 creatures now fill in as you play. The title screen remembers your last calling and difficulty.
    • A redesigned status panel: stat chips, an experience bar, a color-coded journal and item art in your pack.
    • The caves are framed closer, and town and caves remember their own zoom. In Cinematic, dust drifts in the caves and embers rise in the volcano.
    • Menu → Feel & accessibility sets screen shake, reduced flashing, damage numbers, enemy wound bars and motion. The classic rules are unchanged.

    • Auto explore stays off in multiplayer rooms, where every step is shared with your party.

    • When a multiplayer expedition ends, every player gets their own result on the global leaderboard, however long the run was.
    • A finished score the leaderboard refuses no longer holds up the scores after it.
    • The multiplayer Leaderboard shows everyone’s expeditions, with a separate tab for verified co-op runs.
    • Invite links open a filled-in join form with your name and calling, even if you were in another room before.
    • Hosting or joining marks you ready, so the host can start as soon as a friend arrives.
    • The password box only appears for rooms that need one, and host settings sit under Room options.
